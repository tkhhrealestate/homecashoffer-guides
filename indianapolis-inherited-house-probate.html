Inherited an Indianapolis house? What has to happen before anyone can sign a deed

Most families who inherit a house in Marion County don't get stuck on price first. They get stuck on a simpler question: who is allowed to sell it? Until that's answered, a buyer's title company won't close, no matter how good the offer is.

This walk-through is for heirs with a house in Indianapolis or the surrounding townships, whether it's a brick ranch in Lawrence, a double in Fountain Square, or a parent's place in Perry Township that's been empty since the funeral.

Step one: find out how the house was titled

Pull the last recorded deed from the Marion County Recorder before you call anyone else. The answer usually falls into one of three buckets.

  • Transfer on death deed. Indiana allows them under its Transfer on Death Property Act (Indiana Code 32-17-14). If a valid one was recorded, the named beneficiary typically records an affidavit with the death certificate and can sell without a full probate estate for the house.
  • Joint tenants with right of survivorship, or a married couple as tenants by the entirety. The survivor usually already owns it; the paperwork is about clearing the deceased owner's name off title.
  • Only the deceased person's name. This is the common case, and it normally means someone has to be appointed by the court before a deed can be signed.

If there's a living trust, the trustee signs, and probate court may not be involved at all. Check whether the deed was actually moved into the trust; plenty weren't.

Step two: supervised or unsupervised administration

In Marion County, estates are handled by the probate division of the Marion Superior Court. Indiana lets many estates run as unsupervised administration (Indiana Code 29-1-7.5) when the heirs agree and the estate is solvent. The personal representative can then sell real estate without asking the court to approve each step, which saves weeks.

Supervised administration is slower. The court watches more closely, and a sale may need a petition and an order. Disagreement among siblings, a will contest, or an estate that may not cover its debts are the usual reasons it ends up supervised.

Indiana's small estate affidavit is tempting because it skips court, but it's built mainly for personal property. Real estate has its own rules and limits, so ask an Indiana probate attorney before assuming the house can go that route.

Step three: the creditor window

Once a personal representative is appointed, notice goes out to creditors. Under Indiana law, most claims have to be filed within three months after the first publication of that notice (and, in general, no later than nine months after the date of death). Title companies pay attention to this. Some will close a sale inside the window if the estate has enough cash or holds back proceeds; others want to see it run first. Ask before you set a closing date.

The empty-house problem

While the paperwork moves, the house sits. That's where estates quietly lose money:

  • Insurance. Many homeowner policies restrict coverage once a house has been vacant for a set period. Call the carrier, tell them the owner died, and ask whether you need a vacant-dwelling policy.
  • Winter. An unheated house in an Indiana January is a burst-pipe claim waiting to happen. Keep the heat on low or have a plumber winterize it.
  • Property tax installments. Marion County taxes are billed in spring and fall installments. Missing them adds penalties the estate pays at closing.
  • Utilities and lawn. Shut-off notices and tall-grass complaints get mailed to someone who can't open them.

Three questions that tell you whether to list or take cash

Listing usually nets more when the house is in decent shape, the heirs agree, and nobody is paying the carrying costs out of pocket. A cash sale tends to make sense when:

  • the house needs a roof, furnace, or foundation work no heir wants to fund,
  • heirs live out of state and can't manage showings and repairs, or
  • the estate is paying insurance, taxes, and utilities on a house that will sit through a full listing cycle.
