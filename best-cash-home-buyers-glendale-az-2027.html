Arizona rules that change the net sheet

  • No state transfer tax. Arizona does not charge a deed or documentary stamp tax. You still pay recording fees and, on most sales, file an Affidavit of Property Value.
  • Disclosure still matters on an as-is sale. Selling as-is means the buyer accepts the condition you disclose. It does not mean you can hide known material defects.
  • Trustee-sale calendar. Most Arizona residential foreclosures are nonjudicial. After a notice of trustee’s sale is recorded, the sale date must be no sooner than the 91st day (A.R.S. §§ 33-807, 33-808). If you have a sale date, put it on the first call to any buyer.
  • Mortgage-rate backdrop. Freddie Mac’s PMMS put the 30-year fixed rate at 7.18% as of November 5, 2026 (15-year 6.57%). Higher financing costs are one reason cash closings look attractive when a seller needs a firm date.

When a Glendale seller should skip the cash buyers

  • Your house is updated, in a sought-after pocket, and you have time. At 98% of list, an agent listing may net more.
  • Your home is newer and in good shape. Ask for an iBuyer number and an agent’s estimate side by side before you call investors.
  • One repair would open the house to financed buyers. If a single fix (roof, HVAC, clear pest) would let an FHA or conventional buyer get a loan, it may pay for itself.

A cash sale makes more sense if the house needs work you won’t take on, tenants are staying, it’s a vacant inherited property, taxes or an HOA are behind, or you’re on a deadline — including a trustee-sale date.
