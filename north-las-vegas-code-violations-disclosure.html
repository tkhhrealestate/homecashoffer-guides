Selling a North Las Vegas house with an open code case or unpermitted work

Short answer: yes, you can sell. An open code case or an unpermitted patio cover doesn't stop a North Las Vegas sale. It changes who has to fix what, what you have to put in writing, and what a buyer will pay.

The part people get wrong is jurisdiction. North Las Vegas is a separate incorporated city. It isn't the City of Las Vegas, and it isn't unincorporated Clark County, even though all three share the same valley, the same freeways and, often, very similar-looking stucco houses.

Start with whose notice it is

Look at the letterhead on the violation notice. If it came from the City of North Las Vegas, that's the office you deal with for reinspection, fines and any lien it records. Programs you read about for the City of Las Vegas, such as its building amnesty for self-disclosed unpermitted work, belong to that city. Don't assume the same program or fee schedule applies on the north side; call the North Las Vegas building and code offices and ask what they offer before you budget.

If there's an HOA in the picture (common in Aliante, Eldorado and the newer master-planned areas), the association's violation letters are a separate track from the city's. You can have one without the other.

What Nevada makes you disclose, wherever the house sits

The disclosure rules are state law, so they're the same in North Las Vegas, Henderson or Summerlin:

  • Nevada's Seller's Real Property Disclosure form asks whether you know of work done without required permits. A known garage conversion or added casita is a yes.
  • Under NRS 113.130 the form has to be delivered at least 10 days before the property is conveyed, and the buyer can't waive that.
  • Under NRS 113.150 a buyer who learns of a known, undisclosed defect may be able to recover three times the repair cost, plus fees. If you disclose late but before closing, the buyer can generally choose to close or rescind within 4 working days.

The practical takeaway: disclose it up front and let the price reflect it. Hiding a converted garage costs more later than it saves now.

HOA fines are not the same as HOA dues

Nevada limits how an association can collect fines. NRS 116.31162 generally bars an HOA from foreclosing its lien over a fine for breaking the rules, with narrow exceptions for health and safety threats. Unpaid assessments are different and can lead to foreclosure. Either way, fines and balances show up in the resale package, so get the numbers before you sign anything.

Three realistic ways out

  • Cure, then list. Best when the fix is small, like a yard cleanup or a pool that just needs to be drained and treated, and you have the time and cash.
  • Permit after the fact, then list. Worth it for a well-built addition that adds usable square footage, if the city will sign off without tearing walls open.
  • Sell as-is. The buyer takes on the case and the permit work. You give up some price in exchange for no repairs, no reinspection and a closing date you choose.
