Inherited a Houston house? Muniment of title, letters testamentary or a small estate affidavit

Before anyone can sell an inherited house in Houston, a title company has to be satisfied that the person signing the deed has the right to sign it. Texas gives families a few ways to get there. Picking the right one early can save months.

Path 1: Muniment of title

This is a Texas shortcut many families haven't heard of. If there's a valid will and the estate has no unpaid debts other than ones secured by real estate, like a mortgage, a court can admit the will as a muniment of title under Texas Estates Code § 257.001. The court's order serves as the record of who inherits. Nobody gets appointed executor.

Good fit: a paid-off or mortgaged house, a clear will, no credit card or medical debt fights. The catch: the application generally has to be filed within four years of death, and because there's no executor, the heirs named in the will usually all sign the deed. Ask your title company up front whether it will insure a sale on that basis.

Path 2: Letters testamentary or letters of administration

This is regular probate. The court appoints an executor (with a will) or an administrator (without one) and issues letters showing that authority. In an independent administration, the executor can usually sell with less court involvement. In a dependent administration, a sale of real property generally needs court approval.

Good fit: debts to settle, heirs who don't agree, a sibling who can't be reached, or a will that names an executor with power to sell. One person signs, which matters a lot when heirs are spread across several states.

Path 3: Small estate affidavit

This one is narrow. Under Texas Estates Code § 205.001 it's for someone who died without a will, at least 30 days after death, when the assets other than the homestead and exempt property are worth $75,000 or less, and a judge has to approve it. Texas court instructions say the only real estate it can pass is the homestead. A rental house or a second property usually needs Path 1 or 2.

How to choose, quickly

  • Will plus no unsecured debts: ask an attorney about muniment of title first.
  • Debts, disputes, or many heirs to coordinate: letters, so one person can sign.
  • No will, small estate, and the house was the homestead: the small estate affidavit may work.

Whatever the path, the house still costs money while you wait: taxes, insurance, utilities and, in Harris County, flood coverage that a standard policy doesn't include. Texas raised the school homestead exemption in 2025, but that exemption follows a residence homestead, so a vacant estate house may lose it in a later tax year.
