Owning an empty Philadelphia rowhome from out of town: a winter checklist

An empty rowhome in Philadelphia isn't only your problem. It shares a wall, sometimes a roofline, and often a drain with the houses on either side. When the owner lives in the suburbs, another state, or across the country, winter is when small things turn into a neighbor's ruined ceiling and a call to 311. This checklist is for owners who aren't ready to decide anything yet but need the house to survive until spring.

Before the first hard freeze

  • Pick heat or winterize, not neither. Either keep the heat on at a safe minimum with someone checking it, or have a plumber drain the lines and shut the water off at the curb. The worst case is gas off and water on.
  • Clear the roof drains and gutters. Flat and low-slope rowhome roofs pond when drains clog. Water that sits on the roof often finds the party wall first and shows up in the neighbor's bedroom before yours.
  • Call your insurer and say the word "vacant." Many standard homeowners policies restrict some coverage once a home sits empty for a set period. Get the answer in writing.
  • Give both next-door neighbors your number. They'll notice a leak, a broken back window, or an open alley door long before you do.

Know where you are on the City's clock

Philadelphia attaches rules to a vacant house on a schedule, and winter is when owners usually cross one of the lines without noticing.

  • Within 45 days of moving out: if the house had the Homestead Exemption and is no longer your primary residence, the Department of Revenue says to notify it within 45 days.
  • Around three months empty: L&I ties its Section 9-3905 notice to a property vacant three or more months with no construction permit or license on record. The fix is a Vacant Residential Property License, which the City lists at $202 and which renews yearly. Fees change, so check phila.gov.
  • Any time: the property maintenance code (PM-901) expects a vacant building to be free of trash, with doors, windows and roof openings in repair and reachable openings secured.
  • After 12 months not legally occupied: Pennsylvania's Act 135 lets a qualifying party petition for a conservator if other blight conditions are met, and one condition is no good-faith marketing in the prior 60 days.

Open the mail you've been avoiding

Vacant houses collect small debts. A Philadelphia title search will find them, so it's better to see them now. Look up water and sewer liens by address on the City's water lien search, and note that the City asks for a water payoff request at least 30 days before settlement. Check for delinquent real estate tax years, any L&I code-enforcement judgments, and PGW, which can file liens for unpaid gas bills. Unpaid taxes and water bills can eventually send a property to a tax sheriff sale, which is separate from a mortgage foreclosure. If a mortgage is also behind, the foreclosure timeline by state shows how Pennsylvania's process compares.

Then decide on paper

Holding is reasonable if you can keep the house licensed, insured, heated and checked weekly, and you plan to live in it, rent it, or renovate it. Listing can net more if the house shows well on a block where buyers compete. An as-is cash sale tends to fit when a roof or party-wall leak has started, liens or violations are stacking up, or managing an empty rowhome from far away just isn't working.
