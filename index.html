Wake County put your Raleigh house in The News & Observer. What a tax lien, HOA claim, and judgment actually mean when you sell

The letter from Wake County Tax Administration is short and hard to ignore: unpaid real-estate taxes will be advertised in The News & Observer unless the balance is paid. For a lot of Raleigh owners we hear from, that notice lands on top of an HOA claim of lien at the Clerk of Superior Court, a contractor who never got a final payment, or a medical judgment from a rough year. None of those automatically ends a sale — they do change who gets paid at closing, and how fast you need to move.

This is written for the owner of a 1980s North Raleigh townhouse off Falls of Neuse or Six Forks, a brick ranch near Millbrook, or a Cary / Garner / Knightdale house still on a Wake County tax account. Mortgage escrow stopped years ago, so the July bill is yours — and after two summers slipped by, the county letter arrived.

What the News & Observer advertisement actually starts

North Carolina requires every county to advertise unpaid tax liens on real property each year. Under G.S. 105-369, the tax collector mails the record owner a notice at least 30 days before the ad runs, and the advertisement has to publish sometime between March 1 and June 30. Wake County runs its list in The News & Observer and describes that ad as the first step in foreclosure.

Two practical takeaways:

  • Paying the full balance before publication keeps your name out of the paper. A partial payment does not.
  • Interest and costs keep growing after the ad — leaving your name off the list does not waive what you still owe.

Wake collects county and City of Raleigh (and most other Wake towns) on the same account, so one unpaid bill can cover more than one taxing unit. If the ad already ran, you can still sell.

How Wake County interest and collection really work

Under G.S. 105-360, property taxes are due September 1 and can be paid at face value through January 5. After that:

  • From January 6 through February 1, interest of 2% is added.
  • From February 1 on, interest runs at three-quarters of one percent per month (or any fraction of a month) until paid.

That interest schedule is the same statewide. What surprises owners who moved to Raleigh from elsewhere is what Wake can do next. The county says enforced collection may begin as soon as an account is delinquent, and the tools it lists include wage garnishment; attachment of bank accounts, rental income, or income-tax refunds; seizure and sale of personal property by the Sheriff; or foreclosure of the real estate. Each step adds costs to the bill. A bank attachment can hit the same account you were using for HOA dues.

North Carolina does not sell tax-lien certificates to investors the way Florida does. The county itself forecloses, either mortgage-style under G.S. 105-374 or in rem under G.S. 105-375. After the auction, a 10-day upset-bid period follows (minimum raise: 5% of the current bid or $750, whichever is greater). You can generally stop the process by paying all taxes, interest, and costs before the sale is confirmed or the upset-bid window ends. Wake's foreclosure page notes Sheriff auctions often land roughly four months after a judgment is docketed — if you already have a sale date, put it on the first call to any buyer.

HOA, contractor, and judgment liens hide at the Clerk — not the Register of Deeds

Pulling only your deed at the Wake County Register of Deeds will miss the liens that most often stack on a Raleigh townhouse.

  • HOA assessment liens (G.S. 47F-3-116). An assessment unpaid 30 days or more becomes a lien when the association files a claim of lien with the Clerk of Superior Court (after mailing you a statement at least 15 days earlier). The board may vote to foreclose under power of sale once the assessment stays unpaid 90 days or more. A lien made up only of fines (or only of certain fees) can be enforced only through judicial foreclosure. If the association never starts enforcing within three years of filing, the lien is extinguished. Property taxes still outrank it.
  • Contractor / mechanics' liens. A contractor who improved the property generally has 120 days after last furnishing labor or materials to file a claim of lien on real property with the Clerk. A heat-pump or roof job that never got a final check often shows up here.
  • Judgment liens (G.S. 1-234). A money judgment docketed in Wake County is generally a lien on real estate you own in that county for ten years from entry, and on property acquired later. Out-of-county judgments can be brought in by transcript.

A North Carolina title search covers all of these — guessing your own payoff is how sellers get surprised at closing.

What the settlement statement usually shows

North Carolina residential closings are typically run by a real estate attorney. On the seller's side, expect lines for delinquent Wake County and city taxes (interest and collection costs included), a proration of the current year's bill, the HOA payoff, contractor or judgment payoffs, the customary deed excise tax of $1 per $500 of the price, and any mortgage payoff. What's left is yours.

Two Wake quirks to flag early: if a parent was on the Circuit Breaker tax-deferment program, Wake County says the last three years of deferred taxes become payable with interest when a disqualifying event — including a transfer — happens, so get that number from Tax Administration before you lock a price. And if you sell after the July bill mails but before January delinquency, the county may send the new owner a statement for the unpaid tax — your attorney settles it on the settlement statement.

Listing vs. an as-is cash sale when liens are stacked

Listing still wins when the townhouse shows well, the tax and HOA balances are modest next to your equity, and you have months before garnishment or a Sheriff sale date. A direct cash sale tends to fit when collection has already reached a paycheck or bank account, a tax foreclosure judgment is docketed, several Clerk liens are stacked, the house needs work a lender won't finance, or you simply need one firm closing date with no showings.
